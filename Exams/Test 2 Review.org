* Section 4.3  Subspaces of $R^3$
** Checking if a vector space is a subspace
A subspace can be verified by checking if the set has clousure under vector addition and scalar multiplication.

*** Example 1

\begin{align*}
(a) \quad W = {(x,y) : y = ax}
\end{align*}

Sol.

\begin{align*}
(x_1, y_1) + (x_2, y_2) \\
(x_1, ax_1) + (x_2, ax_2) \\
(x_1, x_2, a(x_1 + x_2)
\end{align*}

---

\begin{align*}
c(x, y) \\
c(x, ax) \\
(cxn, cax) \\
(cx, a(cx))
\end{align*}

*** Example 2

\begin{align*}
W = {(x, y) : y = ax^2}
\end{align*}

Sol.

Fails clousure of scalar multiplication:

\begin{align*}
c(x, ax^2) \\
(cx, c(ax^2) \neq (cx, a(x^2)))
\end{align*}

* Section 4.4 - Spanning Sets and Linear Independence
** Determining if a set of vectors spans
S spans $R^2$ means any vector in $R^n$ can be written as a linear combination of vectors in $S$.

$S$ is $L.I$ means that if $\vec{0}$ can be written as a linear combination of vectors in $S$ in a unique way.

\end{align*}
